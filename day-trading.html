You asked to get rich fast and not think. I'm going to give you a system that removes the thinking — but I won't lie to you about the "fast rich" part, because that lie is exactly what empties small accounts.

The four hard truths

  • The PDT rule. A US margin account under $25,000 can only make 3 day trades per rolling 5 business days. With $100 you are heavily rate-limited. (A cash account avoids this but you must wait for funds to settle.)
  • The math of small accounts. Even a great trader risking 1% per trade is risking $1 on a $100 account. To "get rich" you'd need hundreds of perfect compounding trades with zero large losses. One bad day undoes weeks.
  • Costs are brutal at this size. Spreads, slippage, and any fees are a fixed tax that's tiny on $25k and enormous on $100.
  • ~70–90% of active day traders lose money over time. The edge isn't picking winners — it's risk control and repetition. That's learnable. Getting rich on $100 in weeks is not realistic.

What $100 is genuinely great for

  • A flight simulator with real emotions. Small enough that a wipeout teaches you, not bankrupts you.
  • Building one repeatable edge — the only thing that compounds.
  • Proving you can follow rules before you ever risk real size.

My honest recommendation: run this whole thing in PAPER mode for 30 trading days first. Same skills, same data, zero risk. If you're net green and disciplined on paper, then the $100 is a graduation fee, not a lottery ticket.

The Mechanical System

One setup. Defined entry, stop, and target. If the setup isn't there, you don't trade. No discretion, no thinking, no FOMO.

Strategy: VWAP Reclaim Pullback (long-only to start)

A liquid stock opens, sells off, then reclaims its VWAP (volume-weighted average price — the intraday "fair value" line every platform draws for free). You buy the first clean pullback that holds above VWAP. It's one of the most documented, beginner-survivable intraday patterns.

Universe (what to even look at)

  • Large, liquid names or ETFs: e.g. SPY, QQQ, AAPL, AMD, NVDA — tight spreads, no gap-and-die penny-stock traps.
  • Price low enough that you can buy at least a few shares with $100 (ETFs/fractional shares help). With $100, focus on paper sizing first; share count is tiny.

The exact entry checklist (all must be true)

  • It's between 9:45–11:00 AM ET (skip the chaotic first 15 min and the lunch chop).
  • Price is above VWAP and has pulled back to VWAP without breaking decisively below it.
  • A green candle closes back above VWAP (the reclaim). That candle's low is your stop reference.
  • Volume on the reclaim candle is not dead (≥ the prior few candles).
  • You have day trades remaining (PDT) and haven't hit your daily loss limit.

The exits (decided BEFORE you enter)

  • Stop: a few cents below the reclaim candle's low. This defines your risk. The calculator turns it into share size.
  • Target: 2R — twice your risk distance. Sell the whole position there.
  • Time stop: if it hasn't hit target or stop in ~30 min and is going nowhere, exit flat. Capital is for setups, not hope.
  • Hard rule: never move your stop down/away. Ever. That single rule outperforms most "strategies."

The guardrails (non-negotiable)

  • Risk per trade: max 2% of the account (the calculator enforces this).
  • Daily loss limit: 2 losing trades = done for the day. Walk away. This is the rule that keeps you alive.
  • Daily profit lock: up a clean win? Banking it and stopping is allowed and smart.
  • No revenge trades, no adding to losers, no "just this once."

How To Actually Start (free, today)

Step 1 — Paper trade (do this first, no money)

  • Open a free paper-trading / simulated account (most major brokers and TradingView offer one). You get real live charts and fake money.
  • Add the VWAP indicator to a 1- or 2-minute chart of SPY or QQQ.
  • Trade only the checklist setup in "The System." Log every result in the Journal tab.

Step 2 — Grade yourself after 30 trades

  • Are you net green? Is your "rules followed" rate near 100%?
  • If yes → the $100 becomes a small live test of execution under real emotion, not a get-rich bet.
  • If no → keep paper trading. You just saved $100 by finding out for free.

Step 3 — If/when you go live with $100

  • Use a broker with $0 commissions and fractional shares so $100 can actually take a position.
  • Consider a cash account to sidestep the PDT 3-trade limit (trade-off: wait for settlement).
  • Same rules. Same calculator. Same 2-losses-and-done. The size changed; the discipline didn't.

Realistic expectation: at 2% risk, a good day is a few dollars. Compounding is slow and real, not instant and magical. The win here is becoming someone who can run a system — that skill scales to real capital later.
